# Research Brief (cross-cutting): Entrainment for Awake Modules

**Status: Approved 2026-11-11.** Applies to Quit Smoking, Gratitude, Inner Coherence, and Loving Compassion.

## Evidence

| Finding | Source | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| Binaural beats have a modest pooled effect on anxiety, attention, memory, and pain. Exposure *before and during* a task works better than during only, and longer exposure helps. Masking beats with noise does not reduce the effect. | Garcia-Argibay, Santed & Reales 2019, *Psychol Res*: 22 studies, 35 effect sizes, g=0.45 | **Moderate** for small subjective effects; heterogeneous and publication-bias prone |
| EEG entrainment by binaural beats is inconsistent: 5 of 14 studies supportive, 8 contradictory, 1 mixed | Ingendoh, Posny & Heine 2023, *PLoS One* | **Contradicted / mixed** for the mechanism |
| Anxiety reductions do not depend on the specific beat frequency (6, 10, and 25 Hz performed alike) | 2025 crossover study (Taiwan) and similar small RCTs | **Weak**. Suggests a non-specific relaxation and expectancy effect. |
| Memory and attention: g=0.40, but theta and beta results conflict | 2023 meta-analysis (15 studies, 31 effect sizes) | **Weak–moderate** |
| Amplitude-modulated (isochronic/monaural) tones drive cortical steady-state responses more strongly than binaural beats | Schwarz & Taylor 2005; Draganova 2008; Pratt 2010 *Hear Res*; Orozco Perez 2020 *eNeuro* | **Moderate** for a cortical response; no evidence that this produces psychological benefit |
| Binaural beats are audible mainly with low carriers (below ~1 kHz); comfort ratings fall as carrier frequency rises | Licklider 1950; Schütze, Kirsch & Reber replication | **Moderate** |
| 40 Hz beats improved attention without measurable entrainment | Exp Brain Res 2021 | **Weak** |

## User feedback (aggregate)
- Binaural and brainwave apps (Brainwave, BrainAural, Atmosphere, and others; thousands of App Store reviews) show the same recurring complaints: harsh or "buzzing" tones with no masking, fatigue from high carriers, needing headphones, and abrupt starts and stops. Praise centres on a tone layered softly under noise or music.
- Users report "feeling it" at many frequencies, which is consistent with the frequency-independent anxiety findings above, so expectancy is likely a large part of it.

## Recommendations
- Treat beats as a **supporting layer**: quiet (≈18 dB under noise), masked, and optional.
- Carrier **220 Hz** for awake modules (comfortable, clearly audible beat, consistent with the sleep module's 200–250 Hz).
- Use **alpha (≈10 Hz)** for settling and relaxed alertness, **light theta (6–7 Hz)** during absorption and imagery, and **low beta (14–16 Hz)** for the last 60–90 s so every awake session finishes alert.
- **Isochronic fallback** without headphones: the same beat rate as amplitude modulation on a single carrier, smoothed (raised-cosine) pulses to avoid clicks, and kept even quieter because pulsed sound is more intrusive. Carries a seizure caution.
- **Self-experiment:** randomise sessions to beats on or off so each user can see whether beats matter *for them*. This is the honest answer to mixed evidence.

## Conflicts
- Users strongly believe in specific frequencies, while research shows frequency-specific effects are weak. *Resolution:* keep frequency choices evidence-informed and say plainly in-app that beats are optional and experimental.
